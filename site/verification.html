What this audit changed, including the parts that looked worse

Three governance databases were added on 24 November, after the nightly full backup had already run. They were online, constrained and queryable — and they had no backup history at all. The scheduled log job then failed every 15 minutes because it cannot back up a log with no full backup behind it. The health check caught exactly that and returned CRITICAL; it was not silenced. Full backups were taken for all nine, VERIFYONLY passed 9/9, the three databases were restored to scratch copies with identical row counts, and the check returned 46 OK. Both states are published above.

The public numbers were also rebuilt from one inventory rather than retyped: 64 application tables (the six dbo.sysdiagrams support tables are excluded), 46 foreign keys, 11 views and nine application procedures. Backup counts are stated with a timestamp because msdb grows every 15 minutes; a fixed total would be wrong by lunchtime. The 6/6 backup badge, the 24-discipline count and the 202-versus-217 split were three different scopes being printed as one.
