Investigation P-02 · Telemetry data quality

Oilfield telemetry: missing, late, duplicate and invalid measurements

Missing, late, duplicate, out-of-order and unit-faulted readings staged, quarantined, corrected and scored against the cadence the PLC actually ran at.

1. Industry context

A wellhead PLC exposes pressure, flow and temperature. A collector reads it over Modbus and writes to a historian. Between the sensor and the historian, readings are dropped, delayed, duplicated, reordered, mis-united and occasionally corrupted. Operations then reason over that data.

2. Documented problem

  • Goknil et al. (2023), ACM Computing Surveys 55(14s):327 — the most-addressed defects are outliers, missing values, noise, timeliness and inconsistency; the review found no metric for orderliness, traceability, recoverability or auditability.
  • Xia et al. (2025), Information 16(8):701 — timeliness is the common bottleneck across structured, semi-structured and unstructured oil-and-gas data.
  • Klier et al. (2024/2025), BISE 67:797–814 — completeness should be assessed probabilistically rather than against an assumed entity count.
  • Seplat Energy 2024 Integrated Annual Report — IT/OT security is the top-ranked risk, access-rights control is a stated mitigation, and a produced-water calculation error from 2023 was corrected in the 2024 data. No telemetry failure is disclosed.

3. Technical problem

A collector that writes everything it receives to the historian makes invalid, duplicate and mis-united values indistinguishable from good measurements. Corrections overwrite originals, missing periods are inferred from a fixed sample count, and nobody can reconstruct what the sensor actually reported.

4. Laboratory architecture

plc_sim.py (Modbus TCP :15020, 3 holding registers, x100 scaling)
   └─ fault_collector.py  (1 Hz, real Modbus reads, deterministic faults)
        └─ telemetry.Staging  (raw value, raw unit, observed_at, received_at, source)
             ├─ duplicate check  → telemetry.Quarantine
             ├─ unit normalisation + plausibility → telemetry.Quarantine
             └─ accepted → telemetry.Reading (Quality 1/2/3, SourceRunId)
                  └─ telemetry.Correction (original preserved)
                  └─ telemetry.CompletenessScore

8. Corrections

One accepted reading was corrected by +0.1 bar with a reason and an actor. The correction table holds the old value; the view returns OriginalValue from the earliest correction and EffectiveValue from the latest. Both assertions passed (Q11, Q12), and the raw staging row is still present.

9. Root cause of the failures

FaultMechanism
Missingcollector/plant outage and dropped frames; nothing reconstructs them
Duplicateretries and out-of-order collisions with no idempotent key
Out of ordertimestamps taken at the source, arrival order taken at the collector
Unit errordevice configured in a different engineering unit
Spike / corruptsensor fault and malformed frame accepted as a value
Driftcalibration error, invisible without a reference or a trend check

10. Engineering solution

Staging separates arrival from acceptance. Validation normalises units, rejects implausible values, detects duplicate tag/source/timestamp triples, and classifies late and out-of-order arrivals instead of discarding them. Quarantine keeps the raw payload. Corrections preserve originals. Completeness is scored per run from the configured cadence.

11. Limitations

  • Three tags on one simulated wellhead; no OPC-UA, no real SCADA, no multi-site clock skew.
  • The collector and historian are on the same host, so network delay is simulated, not observed.
  • The completeness probability is a simplified gap-tail estimate.
  • Drift is injected, not detected by a model; the lab shows the data-quality consequence, not drift detection.
  • I make no claim about Seplat or any operator's telemetry.

12. Residual risk

Quarantine requires an owner and a review process; otherwise it becomes a silent graveyard. A sustained unit error would be normalised every time and remain invisible unless the rate is alarmed. Corrections are manual, so a correction rate is itself a quality indicator.

13. Production considerations

Validate at the edge where possible; keep the raw frame; alarm on quarantine rate and completeness trend, not just on missing heartbeats; version tag metadata (unit, range) so historical values can be reinterpreted; and never impute an operational measurement without marking it as estimated.

16. What this does not prove

That the pipeline handles real SCADA volume, clock synchronisation across sites, or every industrial protocol. It proves that in this laboratory the listed faults are detected, classified, quarantined or normalised, with original values preserved and completeness measured rather than assumed.
