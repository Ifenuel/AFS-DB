Research and investigations

Four documented industry failures, reproduced on this estate

Each investigation starts with a failure that is documented in the real world, reproduces the mechanism in an isolated laboratory, establishes the cause, implements a control and measures the result. Every number below was produced by executing the scripts in this repository; a case that only has a diagram or an unexecuted script is not counted.

P-01 - Payment integrity and ledger recovery

Documented. A NIBSS Instant Payment fault credited beneficiaries without debiting originators between 6 and 9 September 2024: N13.66bn across 176 accounts in 19 institutions, found in settlement review rather than by the transaction path. Keystone's February 2025 balance inflation followed the same shape at smaller scale.

Reproduced. A processor that commits the debit and the credit separately, has no idempotency constraint, writes balances from a stale read and reconciles nothing. Injected failures produce a debit with no credit, three debits for one request, and two concurrent transfers spending 16,000 from a 10,000 balance.

Fixed. One transaction, a unique idempotency key, a guarded balance update, an in-transaction double-entry invariant, an outbox and an exception queue.

Executed: 31/31 assertions. Baseline reconciliation breaks 1; after the fix 0. Replay of one key produces one debit.

Not claimed: that this is NIBSS's design or root cause, or that it transfers across services or hosts. Full write-up: P-01 payment integrity.

P-02 - Oilfield telemetry data quality

Documented. Industrial data quality degrades mainly through missing, late, noisy and inconsistent readings; the systematic review found almost no metrics for ordering, traceability or auditability.

Reproduced. The existing Modbus simulator is read by a collector that injects dropped samples, duplicates, late and out-of-order timestamps, kPa values, spikes, null frames and a conflicting source. Invalid readings are quarantined, never interpolated.

Fixed. A staging layer with unit normalisation, duplicate detection, plausibility checks, quarantine, corrections that preserve originals, and completeness scored from the run's cadence.

Executed: 13/13 assertions. Staged 265 rows; 21 duplicates and 4 invalid readings quarantined; 240 promoted; about 30 readings estimated missing; lowest completeness ratio 0.867.

Not claimed: that any operator had this failure, or that the simplified gap probability is the BISE/Prophet method. Full write-up: P-02 telemetry quality.

P-03 - Change, failure and recovery

Documented. Clerk's 6 February 2025 outage came from a single manual UPDATE outside staged rollout; the ECB's 27 February 2025 outage was first blamed on DB2 and was actually a storage-hardware fault, and the 60-minute RTO was missed.

Reproduced. On a disposable database, an unbounded UPDATE rewrites 10,000 rows, an unsafe NOT NULL migration is rejected, an open transaction blocks a reader, and the damage is recovered by restoring the full backup plus log backups to a point before the change.

Fixed. A row-count guard that rolls the change back, a nullable/backfill/NOT NULL migration path, READ_COMMITTED_SNAPSHOT for reader blocking, and point-in-time recovery with separate database and verification timings.

Executed: 14/14 assertions. Database restore 0.62 s; reader wait 5.1 s before RCSI and 0.08 s after. Restored checksum matched the baseline exactly.

Not claimed: that database restore time equals service recovery, or that the storage fault was reproduced in hardware. Full write-up: P-03 change and recovery.

P-04 - Privileged access and audit evidence

Documented. Clerk restricted direct database access after its outage; Seplat ranks cyber and IT/OT risk first and discloses access-rights control as a mitigation.

Reproduced. An application principal holding CONTROL on the lab schema rewrites and deletes ledger rows with no in-database evidence of either action.

Fixed. CONTROL revoked, direct DML denied, execution through the payment procedure only, and an audit trigger that attributes every ledger write to the calling principal.

Executed: 11/11 assertions. CONTROL grants 1 to 0; update and delete now denied; 2 ledger writes attributed to the application.

Not claimed: that this replaces SQL Server Audit or a SIEM, or that denied statements are captured by the trigger (they never execute). Case study: P-04 privileged access and audit.

What this does not prove

The laboratories run on one SQL Server instance with synthetic data. They show that specific mechanisms produce specific failures and that specific controls remove them under repeatable tests. They do not show production scale, multi-host behaviour, or any named company's architecture. The research register records each source's credibility, including three payment papers that are conceptual and are used for vocabulary only.
