The job is the worst day, not the demo

One hour of unplanned downtime costs $300,000 or more for 93% of enterprises (ITIC, 2025), and since January 2025 EU financial entities must document and test restoration. Each question below is answered with captured output, not a promise.

Can you get it back?

Every database holds a checksum-verified full backup, logs ship every 15 minutes, and point-in-time recovery was executed on SQL Server and Oracle — a deleted row returned to a chosen second.

391 backup sets · restore compared

Would you know it had stopped?

An hourly health check covers backup age, job outcome and log gaps. It was deliberately broken to confirm it raises CRITICAL and exits non-zero.

hourly run · failure proven

Does it reconcile?

Debit, credit, ledger event and audit row commit together, a request ID makes a replay settle once, and reconciliation returns no unmatched rows across 20,000 transfers.

The problem

Ledger and transaction records drift apart, and recovery is a procedure nobody has run

Most small-bank estates reconcile the ledger, the transfer records and the reporting warehouse by hand at month end, and only discover whether a restore works after data is already lost. This estate closes that gap: reconciliation proves every balance equals its opening balance plus its signed ledger entries across all nine databases, and a recovery drill restores to a point in time and compares the rows before and after.

Reconciliation instead of spot checks

Every transfer writes its debit and credit in one transaction. The reconciliation procedure returns unmatched rows, and it returns none across 20,000 transfers and 40,000 ledger entries.

Recovery, executed and compared

A deletion drill removed 30 support cases; the database was restored to a point before the delete and every removed row was present in the restored copy.

Nine databases, 15 domains

Each domain lists its tables and row counts, aggregated across the databases that own them. Every table is counted once, so the domain rows add up to the estate total; the audit domain spans four databases because each appends to its own audit table. Workload

1,000 customers, 20,000 transfers

The estate carries 1,000 customers, 1,000 accounts and 20,000 transfers posted through the application's own transfer procedure. Every posted transfer carries its two ledger entries and its transfer event, and every account balance reconciles against the ledger.

Customers1,00062 migrated + 500 loaded on Developer
Accounts1,000checking, plus savings for 20%
Transfers20,000posted through the procedure · 40,000 ledger entries
Fraud alerts2,000218 still open

Validation: zero orphan rows across every enabled foreign key, every balance equal to opening balance plus its signed ledger entries, and the application's own reconciliation procedures return no unmatched rows. Payments, settlement batches, support cases and the reporting warehouse were derived from the same transfers. The support-case deletion drill removed 10 cases from 130 and restored all 130 into a point-in-time copy before the copy was dropped, leaving 120 live cases.
