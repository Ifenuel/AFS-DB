Background

Systems thinking, applied where mistakes are expensive

A database either reconciles or it does not. I read it the way I read any system: change one variable, follow the consequence, and defend the conclusion with output rather than opinion. That is why every claim on this site has a capture behind it.

01

Software, built end to end

Two years of interfaces, services, scripts and data flows. Building the application first is why I know how easily it produces rows that nobody can explain later.

02

Then the layer that outlives the feature

Constraints, recovery, privilege and audit. The work below was built schema by schema over several months, and every drill was repeated until it could be reproduced from a clean start.

03

Months on this estate

Nine databases, 64 constrained tables, three engines, encrypted backups, least privilege, access reviews, control testing, a hash-chained archive and two recovery drills. Built schema by schema, not assembled for a screenshot.

04

Next: database administration

I want the role where recovery time, privilege boundaries and audit evidence are the job description rather than an afterthought.

What the months produced

Proof instead of promise

Every number below is generated by a command and kept on the evidence page, including the ones that failed first. That is the standard I want to be held to.

Recovery

A backup is a hypothesis

Restores are drilled to a chosen second, the estate is rebuilt from backup with every row matching, and a warm standby is promoted by a watchdog in 0.6 s, without a human. If it has not been restored, it is not a backup.

Privilege

Reach one database, not nine

The application identity is denied the tables it should not read. Grants are reviewed quarterly and the revoke decision actually removes the grant.

Audit

Evidence that survives the admin

Failed logins are captured with client detail, and archived records are hash-chained so a single altered row is provable rather than suspected.

Honesty

Name the gap

A watchdog promotes the warm standby in 0.6 s while a client sees 4.4 s of failed reads, the TDE protector is rotated with its key held outside the instance, and a Modbus collector records plant data — gaps included. What a lab cannot fake is a hardware HSM, and that is the one absence still named.
